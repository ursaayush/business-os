# Writing Constants — how everything is written, everywhere

> The shared base under everything written for the outside world — posts, emails, video
> scripts, the website, everything. When a draft feels off, check it here first.

## ⭐ The first rule — Simple is genius

**Simplify and humanise, always.** Write like a smart friend talking — not a manual, not a
deck, not a clever line. Plain, everyday words over jargon; short human sentences over
technical or abstract phrasing. If a line sounds *complicated or trying-to-sound-smart*, it's
wrong — cut it down until it sounds like a person saying it out loud. The simplest version
that still carries the whole truth wins, every time.

This rule sits above every other rule in this folder and governs **every word on every
surface** — including how the AI talks to the owner.

## Understate, never hype

Calm, plain, understated English is the one register that works everywhere and reads as
trustworthy. Hype spends trust.

1. **One calm fact beats three excited claims.**
   - ✗ *"This will completely change your life!"* → ✓ *"This changed how I work."*
   - Banned by default: *game-changer · insane · next level · unlock your potential.*
2. **Calibrated claims, never absolutes.** Precision is what a skeptic trusts.
   - ✗ *"This works for everyone, guaranteed."* → ✓ *"It worked for me in six weeks. That was enough to matter."*
3. **Quiet credentials.** State facts; let the reader do the math.
4. **Exclamation marks: effectively zero.** The calm carries.
5. **No idiom or wordplay that needs native-speaker fluency.** Would a smart non-native reader
   follow on the first pass? Then it's good.

## First person first — proof, not conjecture

Lead from lived experience — *"here's what happened to me"* — never assert what the reader
feels or thinks. The friend goes first. Back every claim with something real: a case, a number,
a named source. Never assert what you cannot back.

## The posture

**A friendly guide, not an authority.** Walk alongside the reader and point. Warm, the way you
would talk to a colleague you respect. Never hand-hold, never flatter, never shame.
