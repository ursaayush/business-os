# Green screen — switching your background

> Two ways to replace your background: a **physical green screen** (a green cloth behind you)
> or **software** (the computer finds you and removes the background — no cloth needed).
> Start with software today; add the cheap physical screen when you want crisper results.

## Option A — Software (no green screen needed) · start here

Modern apps can detect a person and cut the background out automatically.

**Easiest: CapCut (free).**
1. Put your clip on the timeline and select it.
2. Look for **"Remove background"** / **"Auto cutout"** (under Video → Cutout).
3. One click — you're floating on nothing. Drag any image or video *under* your clip on the
   timeline: that's your new background.

Also possible in: DaVinci Resolve (Magic Mask — paid Studio version), Final Cut Pro (Scene
Removal Mask). CapCut's is the best free one; use it.

**Make software removal look good** (it's all about contrast):
- Face a window or lamp so **you are well lit**.
- Stand in front of a **plain, uncluttered wall** that doesn't match your clothes.
- Keep **hair tidy** and avoid fuzzy sweaters — wispy edges are what software gets wrong.
- Don't wave objects around; hands holding things confuse the cutout.

**Limits:** edges (hair, fingers) can shimmer, and busy backgrounds confuse it. If you're
seeing artifacts, that's your cue for Option B.

## Option B — Physical green screen (crisper, more professional)

The classic: a green cloth behind you; the editor removes everything green ("keying"). Edges
come out much cleaner than software cutout, and it works in any app.

**Buy (about €30–60 total):**
- A **green screen backdrop** — collapsible pop-up (easiest, springs flat against a wall) or
  a cloth + stand. Any "chroma key green" one online is fine.
- Two cheap **softbox or LED panel lights** if your room is dim. Lighting matters more than
  the screen itself.

**Set up (10 minutes):**
1. **Flatten it.** Wrinkles make shadows, shadows ruin the key. Pop-up screens win here;
   steam or stretch a cloth one.
2. **Light the screen evenly** — one light on each side, aimed at the *screen*, no hot spots.
3. **Light yourself separately** — your light aims at *you*, not the screen.
4. **Stand 1–1.5 m in front of the screen.** This kills your shadow and the green glow
   ("spill") on your shoulders.
5. **Wear no green.** Nothing green, nothing reflective.

**Key it out in your editor:**
- **iMovie:** drag your green-screen clip *above* the background clip on the timeline →
  click the overlay settings button → choose **Green/Blue Screen**. Done.
- **CapCut:** select the clip → **Chroma key** → pick the green with the color-picker →
  raise *Intensity* until the green vanishes cleanly.
- **DaVinci Resolve:** the 3D Keyer on the Color page (more control, more learning curve).

**Fixing a bad key:** green edge around you → lower intensity / add slight edge feather;
patches not disappearing → the screen is unevenly lit, fix the lights, not the software;
green tint on your skin → stand further from the screen.

## Which one, when

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Quick talking-head video, decent light, plain wall | **Software** (CapCut auto-cutout) |
| You want clean, professional edges — or software keeps glitching | **Physical** green screen |
| Filming somewhere you control (your desk corner, every week) | **Physical** — set it up once, leave it standing |
| Filming out and about | **Software** — it travels with you |

Tip: pick one background image/video and stick with it — a consistent backdrop becomes part of
your brand and makes every video instantly recognizable.
